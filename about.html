Our Story

Casa Ciabatta began in 1987 when Marco and Elena Rossi left their jobs in Manhattan to open a small bakery in the neighborhood where they'd grown up. Marco had learned to bake from his grandmother in Verona, and Elena brought her family's recipes for cured meats and antipasti. What started as a 400-square-foot shop with a single oven has grown into the cafe you see today—still family-owned, still using the same sourdough starter Marco brought over from Italy. Their daughter Sofia now runs the front of house while their son-in-law Luca oversees the kitchen, carrying on a tradition that feels as natural as it did on day one.

Why Ciabatta

When we opened, most New Yorkers had never heard of ciabatta—it was a relatively new bread even in Italy, developed in the 1980s. Marco saw something special in it: a bread sturdy enough to hold a loaded sandwich but light enough that the fillings could shine. It became our signature, and eventually our name. Today we bake over 200 loaves a day, each one shaped by hand using the same slow-fermentation method Marco learned three decades ago.

Community & Hospitality

We've watched kids who came in for cookies grow up, go to college, and bring their own families back for Sunday brunch. That continuity means everything to us. Casa Ciabatta has always been more than a place to grab lunch—it's where neighbors catch up, where regulars have "their" table, and where everyone is treated like family. We sponsor the local little league, host an annual block party, and keep a pot of coffee on for anyone who needs a place to sit for a while.
